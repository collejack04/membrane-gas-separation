Purity vs recovery tradeoff — live

Each point on the blue curve is a different stage cut, at your current selectivity, feed composition and pressures. The red dot is where you are now. You cannot get both high purity and high recovery in one stage — that is the point.

At low stage cut you skim off only the fastest gas, so purity is high but you recover little. At high stage cut you force almost everything through, so recovery is high but the slow gas comes with it and purity falls to near the feed composition.

How the math works — in 60 seconds

1. Flux. For each gas i: Ji = Qi × (pfeed·xi − pperm·yi). Q is permeance, the bracket is the partial-pressure driving force. A is the fast gas.

2. Selectivity. α = QA / QB = PA / PB. Only the ratio matters for separation. Absolute Q matters for area.

3. Permeance vs permeability. Permeance [GPU] = Permeability [Barrer] / thickness [µm]. Same material, thinner layer, more flux, same selectivity.

4. Balances. Overall: F = P + R. On A: F·xF = P·y + R·x. Stage cut: θ = P / F. Recovery: θ·y / xF.

5. The model. Complete-mixing (Weller–Steiner): both sides are well mixed, so the flux ratio gives y/(1−y) = α·(x − r·y) / ((1−x) − r·(1−y)) with r = pperm/pfeed. Substitute x from the balance and solve for y by bisection. Full derivation: docs/theory.md. Python reference: src/membrane.py — the JavaScript in assets/app.js is the same solver.

Why one stage is not enough. Even at α = 30, 15% feed only reaches ~55% purity at very low recovery. Real plants use two stages, a recycle, or a higher α membrane. That is a good “what I want to learn next” for the presentation.
